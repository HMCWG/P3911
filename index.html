Standard for Holographic Video Coding

IEEE P3911 defines how holographic video is represented, coded and reconstructed for 6DoF viewing. The i3DV reference software validates the tools that enter the standard.

The standard defines a set of tools for efficient representation and encoding of holographic video, the corresponding definitions of syntax, semantics, parsing, and decoding procedures. The standard addresses holographic video that supports both 6 Degrees of Freedom (6DoF) interaction and photorealistic virtual viewpoint rendering. The holographic video content includes multi-view video sequences, multi-view video plus depth, and neural representation like three-dimensional and four-dimensional (3D/4D) Gaussian splatting. The target applications and services include immersive communication, education, entertainment, and healthcare.

01 — About the project

Scope, working group and reference software

IEEE P3911 specifies a set of tools for efficient representation and encoding of holographic video, together with the syntax, semantics, parsing and decoding procedures that make a bitstream decodable. It addresses content supporting 6 Degrees of Freedom (6DoF) interaction and photorealistic virtual-viewpoint rendering: multi-view video, multi-view video plus depth, and neural representations such as three-dimensional and four-dimensional (3D/4D) Gaussian splatting.

The project is developed in the IEEE C/DC Holographic Media Coding Working Group (HMCWG). Its PAR was approved on 10 December 2025, with the first Standards Committee ballot scheduled for December 2027 and RevCom submission for October 2029. The P3911 meeting series begins with Meeting 1 in March 2026; the seven earlier records, from June 2024 to December 2025, predate the project.

i3DV is the reference software of the project. Tools adopted at a working-group meeting are integrated into a numbered i3DV release, from 1.0 to 8.0, and those releases are used to verify coding efficiency and decoder behaviour across the common test sequences and conditions. The software serves the standard; it is not a separate standardization effort.
